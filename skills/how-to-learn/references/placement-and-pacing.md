# Placement and pacing

## Placement test (about 15 minutes per subject)

Closed book, no help. Go through the steps in order and stop at the first one the learner can't do: that is the starting level. If they clear all six, they are past this ladder for this subject.

1. **Recall:** Write everything you know for 10 minutes. Fewer than 5 solid, checkable facts means start at Level 1.
2. **Explain:** Explain it in writing or aloud for 5 minutes. Could a smart 12-year-old follow it? If not, start at Level 2.
3. **Use:** Solve one new problem or task with no reference. If you can't, start at Level 3.
4. **Diagnose:** Compare two approaches to one problem, or find the flaw in a sample solution. If you can't, start at Level 4.
5. **Judge:** Review a study or work plan and say what to keep, cut, or change, with reasons. If you can't justify the changes, start at Level 5.
6. **Create:** Design a two-week plan for someone at your level, or coach them once. If you can't, start at Level 6.

Before the first session, everyone sets up foundations: a weekly schedule and protected sleep.

## Pacing

- **Sessions per week:** default 4. Minimum useful: 2. Maximum sustainable: 8. If more than 8 is needed, check the approach first.
- **Time split:** about 70% on the current level, 20% on spaced review of earlier levels, 10% on predict-then-score practice (guess your result before you check it).
- **Spacing:** the longer you need to remember something, the longer the gap should be, but a smaller share of that time. In one study (Cepeda et al., 2008) the best gap was about 20-40% of a one-week delay and about 5-10% of a one-year delay. As a rule of thumb: to remember for a week, review after 1-3 days; to remember for a year, review after 2-5 weeks. Treat these as starting points and adjust to your own recall.
- **Session shape (60 min default):** 30 practice, 15 reading, 15 self-test and review. Scale to the time available. Record scores and errors.
- **Review schedule:** review new material after about 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, then 1 month. Stretch the next gap when recall is easy, shorten it when the learner misses. Record the next review date in the progress file.
- **Difficulty:** tasks should be hard but doable. A good target is roughly 85% correct (Wilson et al., 2019, a computational study, so treat it as a guide). If you get more than half wrong two sessions in a row, drop one level and rebuild. That cutoff is this skill's own rule of thumb, not a research finding.
